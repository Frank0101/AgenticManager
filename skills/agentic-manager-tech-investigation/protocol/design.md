Build the system design from the findings below: enough to draw an accurate diagram of the system as it is today and as the target describes it. Submit the whole design every time: a row of the current design you leave out is removed. Keep a row by giving its ID as its key, [[C03]]; give a new row a new key, [[c1]]. Write every reference in double brackets, [[F07]] or [[c1]]. Each row rests on findings, or on what explains it: a gap, an open decision or a scope decision.

1. Boundaries: what groups components. within is the boundary it sits in, such as a cluster within a cloud account, or None. kind starts with one of: {{boundary_kinds}}, then any detail, such as "cloud account: eu-west-2".
2. Components: every part that runs, stores or talks across a boundary, such as a service, job, library that calls out, store, queue or external part, and the people or roles that act on it. kind starts with one of: {{component_types}}, then any detail. A part is external by how the design knows it, not by what it is: its code can't be read, or isn't researched, so a finding shows it exists, such as "external: Braze", "external: another team's database" or "external: GitHub repository used as a data source". boundary is the boundary it sits in, or None. Challenge a generic noun, such as "platform" or "gateway": what runs where, called by whom, and what proves it exists. code links its repository and paths at the pinned revision, or says None. state starts with one of: {{statuses}}. A component is in the state its strongest evidence shows. {{precedence}} For an external part, a person, a role or a part of a mention-only topic, 🟩 doesn't say whether {{topic}} uses it yet: its connections' state says that. Any other 🟩 or 🟦 needs among its evidence a finding read in a code source with coverage {{read_coverage}}, at the commit it links, of a path it links, a file under it or, read in full, a folder above it; 🟨 needs a finding read in the tracker among its evidence, 🟪 one read in the documentation.
3. Identities: the credentials or accounts connections use, and what each can reach, citing the components, such as "Retrieve on [[C03]]"; several connections often share one. kind starts with one of: {{identity_kinds}}, then any detail, such as "cloud role: AWS IAM, assumed by a managed service". The script gives each identity the most advanced state of the connections that use it.
4. Connections: who calls whom (from and to, one component each), how, starting with one of: {{connection_kinds}}, then any detail, such as "HTTP: POST /search", with what data, whether the caller waits for a reply (waits_for_reply starts with one of: {{waits}}), with which identity (or None, or Unknown), and its state, as for components.
5. Flows: for each entry point that matters, the connections it uses, in the order they happen: each after the first starts from or ends at a component the flow has already reached, so a part that fetches, such as a consumer reading a queue, joins the flow by its call to what the flow has reached. The script gives each flow the state of its least advanced verified connection, naming any unverified step, such as "🟩 Live, unverified: E01", so the entry point doesn't say it.
6. Unknowns: a component or connection you can't establish is ⬜ Unverified, with the gap that explains it in evidence; a row the target leaves waiting on an open choice cites the decision, [[D01]], beside its findings; a caller you can't identify is a component of kind unknown. Never guess one. A component or connection a finding shows doesn't exist is kept as ✖ Ruled out, citing that finding, rather than left out; a connection to or from it is ruled out too, and no flow uses it.

A component, a connection, an identity and a flow each say in in_the_target how the target changes them, citing the evidence, such as "Replaced by the sync job ([[F12]])" or "Removed once sync runs ([[F14]])", or Unchanged. A row only the target has says what it adds, citing its evidence.

A topic the user decided as mention only is a black box: draw it only where evidence on the side of {{topic}} connects it to {{topic}}, as the boundary {{topic}} runs in, as one component, of the kind that evidence shows, or as both, a boundary and the part of it that calls {{topic}} or is called by it, each named for what it is, such as "Acme platform" and "Acme agent coordinator". Draw no other part of it, except one {{topic}}'s own code calls or uses itself, such as a store its service connects to or the service its manifests are applied by, as a component of its own; draw nothing behind such a part, and nothing {{topic}} reaches only through another part. Each part you draw, and each connection to it, cites the scope decision in its evidence, such as [[U02]], since the user chose not to research it; where the evidence on the side of {{topic}} doesn't establish it, it is ⬜ Unverified. Draw nothing the user excluded, even where a finding names a connection to it.

Build the design only from what the ledger holds, and read no source yourself: reading is research. Where the findings don't establish a row, or are too thin to fill it, raise a question, even when a source already read holds the answer: research reads it again for what the row needs. Raise one only if it could change what the report says about {{aims}}: a component the documents or tickets name whose code nobody read, a connection whose identity, data or direction no source gives, a step of a flow nobody traced, a boundary nothing establishes. Add it in new_questions, with raised_by naming the row or finding that raised it; research answers it before the design is updated. Leave out a row that waits on such a question, such as a component whose code nobody read, and draw it once research answers: ⬜ Unverified is for what research couldn't establish, with the gap that explains it. {{scope}} Raise no question about a topic the user decided as mention only or exclude.
