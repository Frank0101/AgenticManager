# Comment on a person's work

A manager is preparing a performance review of the person named below. Their work over the period, in Jira, GitHub and Notion, has been gathered, and its facts worked out: how much of each kind there was each month, how many areas it touched and which ones most, and how much went to each area. Write a short commentary on each source, for the manager to read before the items themselves.

## Each commentary

A paragraph of about {{commentary_words}} words, at most {{max_commentary_words}}, fewer when there is little to say, on:

- the volume: how much there was, and of what kind;
- the main areas of focus: the epics, repositories or places where most of it went, by their titles;
- any shift over time: volume rising or falling, or the range of work widening or narrowing, month by month;
- every step change: a month where the volume, the range or the main areas change sharply from the months before, such as volume halving or doubling, or the work moving to a new epic. Say when it happened, what changed and by how much, from the facts, and what the facts show coinciding with it, such as a month with little work in every source.

Cite only figures the facts hold, or that add up from them, such as a total. Describe the work, don't judge it: whether the volume or the focus was right is for the manager, who knows the context. Say so when a source holds too little to show a focus or a shift.

How to read the facts:

- Jira: the issues resolved each month, and those touched in the period and still open, by their parent, such as an epic, or "no parent".
- GitHub: the pull requests opened and merged each month, by the parent of the Jira issue that lists them, or by repository when no issue does. Many small pull requests can be a way of working, such as stacking them, rather than more work.
- Notion: the pages created and last edited each month, by where they sit.

{{people}}

For each of `jira`, `github` and `notion`, the paragraph.
