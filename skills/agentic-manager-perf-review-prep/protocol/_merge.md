## How to merge

- What was read about the same behaviour or outcome becomes one point, with the link of everything read about it in `links`: the script adds each one's day. Write a pattern as one: "Jane didn't update the rollout's status, many times", not one point per day. Keep apart points that are about different things, even when they share a value.
- A point that rests on several moments, over weeks or months, of the same thing, such as support for one team or ownership of one system, says what the person did across them as a whole: the kind of contribution, how long or how often, and its effect. A moment's details, such as the bug they fixed, come only as examples, one or two that show what the contribution was, never a list to fill the room. Moments that only share a theme, such as helping different teams with different things, aren't the same thing: keep them apart, or leave out those too small alone.
- A point names only a period its links show, by their days or by what they say, such as a note from November saying "since August". When what you read elsewhere shows when it started, such as a note on the day the person took it over, it belongs to the point: add its link.
- When what was read about the same thing is partly good and partly to improve, such as a habit that changed, make two points, each in its part, never one: the one to improve says when it held, such as "Jane shared progress rarely in July", and the one going well since when, such as "Jane has shared progress every week since September". Weighing one against the other isn't the merge's to do.
- `context` points have `links` too. Merge context that says the same thing into one.
- When nothing is worth keeping, submit every part empty.

When you're in doubt whether something should be a point, leave it out, and list it in `in_doubt` with its day, or its page's title, what it says and why, so the manager can see it. Each line of `in_doubt` is written as a point is.
