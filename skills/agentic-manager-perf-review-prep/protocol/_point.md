Make the person the subject of a point: what they did, or what happened because of them, such as "Jane adds scope nobody asked for", not "The manager praised Jane". What it comes from is the evidence, not the point.

A point is one sentence in your words, about 25 words at most, never copied, that stands on its own: say what it is about, such as a promotion, so it reads without its source.

A reminder or a to-do about the review's process, such as updating objectives before a deadline or keeping evidence for a promotion case, says nothing about the person's work: it isn't a point.

A judgement of the person's character or intentions, such as "has a negative attitude", becomes the behaviours and their effects the source describes; when it describes none, keep it as the source puts it.

A `going_well` point names every company value it clearly shows, and a `to_improve` point every value it calls for, the ones the person would show by doing what it asks: at most two, judged from what it says and each value's meaning, or none when none fits, the one it shows or calls for most first. Consider every value, not only the most common one, and never stretch a point to fit one. For example:

| Point                                                      | Shows or calls for                            | Why                                                      |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Jane rolled out the new on-call process across teams       | Delivery, and challenging how things are done | A new way of working, delivered                          |
| Jane should contribute more directly to the platform work  | Delivering with ownership                     | A `to_improve` point names what doing it would show      |
| Jane works like our principal engineer, though more junior | Working at that level                         | A comparison with someone more senior says something too |

A `context` point names none.
