### Writing a point

Make the person the subject of a point: what they did, or what happened because of them. When it is someone's judgement, say whose, such as "Jane's review saved the release, as Sam put it", not "Sam praised Jane's review".

A point is one sentence in your words, never copied, that stands on its own: say what it is about, such as a promotion, so it reads without its source. It is up to {{point_words}} words, fewer when the point is simple.

A judgement of the person's character or intentions, such as "has a negative attitude", becomes the behaviours and their effects the source describes; when it describes none, keep it as the source puts it. A judgement that continues a behaviour described elsewhere joins that point: write the point from the behaviour, and say whose the judgement is.

{{people}}

For example:

| The source says                                                                                                  | The point                                                                                                          | Why                                                                     |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The manager wrote that Jane was the only one who gave them the quarter's plan on time, though not done very well | "Jane was the only one to hand the manager the quarter's plan on time, though not done very well", not "delivered" | "Gave me" is handing over, not doing the work; the quality caveat stays |

### Values

A `going_well` point names every company value it clearly shows, and a `to_improve` point every value it calls for, the ones the person would show by doing what it asks: at most {{max_values}}, judged from what it says and each value's meaning, or none when none fits, the one it shows or calls for most first. Consider every value, not only the most common one, and never stretch a point to fit one. For example, with values invented here, not the company's:

| Point                                                      | Values                  | Why                                                      |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Jane rolled out the new on-call process across teams       | Initiative, Reliability | A new way of working, delivered                          |
| Jane should contribute more directly to the platform work  | Initiative              | A `to_improve` point names what doing it would show      |
| Jane works like our principal engineer, though more junior | Craft                   | A comparison with someone more senior says something too |

### Context

A `context` point is a fact the review must take into account to read the period fairly, such as an absence, a change of role, team or manager, a responsibility taken on, support given or needed, or a development plan or a promotion case. It names no value. The work the person did isn't context, as the prep gathers it from their tools, nor are logistics, such as meeting times.
