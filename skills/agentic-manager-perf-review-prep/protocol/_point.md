Make the person the subject of a point: what they did, or what happened because of them, such as "Jane adds scope nobody asked for", not "The manager praised Jane". The note it comes from is the evidence, not the point.

A point, and each line of `in_doubt`, is one sentence in your words, about 25 words at most, never copied, that stands on its own: say what it is about, so it reads without its source. Write the person's name, or "they", and for anyone else their name, their role, such as "the office manager", or "they": never "he" or "she", nor "his", "her" or any other gendered pronoun.

Keep a personal fact only as far as it bears on the work, and leave out personal reasons, such as health or family: "Jane asked for an exemption from the office rule", not why.
