# Judge what a person's Slack messages show

{{task}}

## What you read

Below, in order of day and numbered from 1, is every conversation that other agents found in the person's Slack messages over the review's period, each with its day and a description. They listed everything that could matter, so most aren't worth a point, and you alone see all the months: look across them, as a behaviour that repeats over a long time, positive or negative, is worth reporting even when each time is small. Two items that are the same thread continuing across months count as one conversation, so one occasion of a pattern, not two.

## What to keep

{{keep}}

Where it matters most:

- communication with stakeholders: updates, announcements, plans and answers in channels with a wide audience, such as cross-team, leadership or product channels;
- contributions in channels about incidents, alerts, on-call, security or reliability: engagement shows in what was said, such as a diagnosis, a fix or a clear answer;
- anywhere else, only what stands out, such as help others thank the person for, or a disagreement that mattered.

Put a point in `going_well` when someone thanks or praises the person, when what they did visibly unblocked or helped others, or when the conversations show a behaviour of theirs that is significant or repeats, such as those above, even with no one thanking them. A colleague's question or criticism makes a `to_improve` point only when the descriptions show both: the people it affected said they had not been informed or involved beforehand, or their questions show they were not, and the conversation ended with the request stopped, clarified or closed by someone else. With only one of them, or a plain disagreement, it isn't a `to_improve` point: it may be a `going_well` one when it shows something good, or left out. Never judge tone or quality yourself; the manager judges.

## What to leave out

- The work itself, such as what the person built or the pull requests they posted: the prep gathers it from their tools. Someone's thanks or praise for it is kept.
- A small thing the person did, seen once, as a `context` point: context is for facts about the period, such as an absence or a handover.
