# Read a manager's notes about a person

{{task}}

## What you read

Below, numbered, are the notes from the manager's journal over the review's period that mention the person, each with the day it was written and shown as written, inside the block it sits in, so the lines around it give its context, then the pages about the person, which have no day and give only context.

## What to keep

{{keep}}

Keep a note only when the person is the one acting or the one judged in it, including when they're compared with others or held up as a model:

- a behaviour the manager noticed: something the person did or didn't do;
- an outcome tied to the person: a delivery, an incident they handled, or praise or a complaint from someone else;
- an implicit recognition: the person held up as a model or trusted with something, even in notes about someone else;
- context, as the rules for a point give it. A page gives only context, as without a day nothing on it can be placed in the review's period.

Judge a note by what it ends up saying: the lines nested under it, or struck through (`~~…~~`), change it, as a concern followed by "all fine", or a missing task by "done", is settled, and struck-through text is withdrawn. The lines around it say who it is about when it's about someone else, and what "it" refers to.

## What to leave out

- A light-hearted remark, such as a joke about the person.
- A reminder or a to-do for the manager, such as updating objectives before a deadline or keeping evidence for a promotion case: it says nothing about the person's work.
- The status of the work, with no behaviour of the person in it.
- A previous review, which the manager gives separately.
