# Summarise what is going well, what to improve and the context

{{opening}}

It holds the person, the company's values with what each means, `focus`, where most of the person's pull requests and resolved issues went, and each table's points in its order, each with its source and when it was seen and, in going well and to improve, the values it names, the first the one it is grouped under.

## What to write

The report shows three parts: what is going well, what to improve and the context, the points of the first two grouped under the company value they show most. It shows what you write over the points: in going well and to improve, a short summary for each value worth saying, with that value's points folded under it, and a line on the points under no value that matter, with those points under it; in context, a summary of its own. The points no summary covers come folded last.

## How to write going well and to improve

- Choose the values worth saying: those whose points show something that matters for the review, not every value the table holds. A value with a single minor point can stay among the points folded last.
- For each, say what the person did, as behaviours, from its points alone: what they show together, the main contribution first, never a list of the points. Name no value inside the text: the report shows its name above it.
- Judge what matters by how often a theme comes back across the points and by `focus`: a measure of volume, not of impact, so it orders what the points say and adds nothing they don't.
- Say once what several points, or several sources, show together. A detail of one moment comes only as an example, never a list to fill the room.
- A point that changed over time stays as the points put it: in going well, since when it holds; in to improve, when it held. Weighing one against the other isn't yours to do.
- In to improve, say what was seen, what the person should do, and how progress would show, as the points give it.
- `also` is a line on the points under no value that matter, such as an overall judgement of the person's level; leave it null when there are none, or none worth saying.
- {{style}}
- Write about {{value_words}} words a value, and the same for `also`, fewer when the points are few: never pad.
- Leave `values` empty, {}, and `also` null, when the table holds no point.

## How to write the context

The context summary isn't about behaviours or values: it gives the facts the review must take into account to read the period fairly, such as absences, changes of role, team or manager, responsibilities taken on or handed over, support given, a development plan or a promotion case. Say each fact once, even when several sources say it, the weightiest first. Name no company value and pass no judgement on the person: the context explains the period, the other two summaries weigh it. The rules above on writing in the third person and in flowing prose still apply, but at about {{context_words}} words, fewer when there is little to say.
