# Judge and merge what a manager's notes say about a person

A manager is preparing a performance review of the person named below. Other agents read the manager's notes a month at a time, and the person's pages, and wrote a synopsis of every note that mentions the person, with its day; a page has none. Most notes aren't feedback. Decide what in them is, and write it as one summary of what the notes say about the person.

## What to keep

The review needs the picture over the whole period, not every detail. Keep only what has a clear impact or is a significant signal about the person; a small thing, such as a minor slip, a one-off thanks or a routine task, stays out unless it repeats into a pattern, which is then one point.

Keep a note only when the person is the one acting or the one judged in it, including when they're compared with others or held up as a model:

- a behaviour the manager noticed: something the person did or didn't do;
- an outcome tied to the person: a delivery, an incident they handled, or praise or a complaint from someone else;
- an implicit recognition: the person held up as a model or trusted with something, even in notes about someone else;
- context: a fact that is neither good nor bad, such as leave, a change of team or a promotion conversation. A page gives only context, as without a date nothing on it can be placed in the review's period.

Judge a note by what it ends up saying, as its synopsis gives it.

## What to leave out

- A small thing seen once, with no clear impact.
- A light-hearted remark, such as a joke about the person.
- A reminder or a to-do for the manager.
- A note where the person is only a name in passing, or a reference for something else.
- The status of the work, with no behaviour of the person in it.

When you're in doubt whether a note should be a point, leave it out, and list it in `in_doubt` with its day, or its page's title, what it says and why, so the manager can see it.

## How to merge

- Notes about the same behaviour or outcome become one point, with every day it was seen on in `dates`, oldest first. Write a pattern as one: "Didn't update the rollout's status, many times", not one point per day. Keep apart points that are about different things, even when they share a value.
- When notes about the same thing disagree, keep both as points, or say how it changed, such as "Shared progress rarely in July, every week from September".
- `context` points are sentences alone, with no dates. Merge context that says the same thing into one.
- When nothing is worth keeping, submit every part empty.
